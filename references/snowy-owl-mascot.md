# Snowy owl mascot

Read and apply this reference only when the current project has explicitly selected a snowy owl as its series character. Do not make the snowy owl the default character for all Xiaohongshu projects.

## Core construction

- Keep the core appearance consistent across the series: a rounded white head, black-and-gray markings, and yellow eyes.
- Use a cartoon snowy owl as the primary design language, while varying poses to fit each card instead of copying one pose throughout the set.
- Adjust expression, clothing, and props when the role or scene requires it without changing the core identity.
- When the owl performs an action, draw exactly two clear black stick-figure arms.
- Treat the stick-figure arms as an additional action system. Never use the wings directly as hands.
- Keep the body, arms, and hand actions visually distinct.

## Clothing and standing anatomy

- When the owl wears clothing, show no oval wings or exposed feather patches outside the garment.
- Make the clothing fully cover the torso. Never add oval wings that appear to grow out of the clothing.
- Keep scarves outside the clothing or body; never let a scarf emerge from inside a garment.
- In a full-body standing pose, include two short legs and clearly grounded claws.
- Give the standing body a supported silhouette. Do not shape it like a bowling pin, capsule, or unsupported oval column.

For a clothed standing owl, include this exact structural constraint in the image prompt:

> exactly two black stick-figure arms, no visible oval wings, clothing fully covers the torso, scarf stays outside the clothing, two short legs and grounded claws when standing.

## Roles, props, and composition

- Let the owl play a psychologist, product manager, researcher, user, or another role when the content calls for it.
- Establish role identity through glasses, clothing, notebooks, documents, furniture, professional tools, and expression.
- Use only props that clarify the content. Do not add unrelated objects merely to enrich the picture.
- Use bright color when appropriate, but control saturation to avoid a harsh or glaring result.
- Place the owl according to the current card's narrative function and visual flow. Do not keep it in the lower-left corner across the series.
- Preserve the same character construction and series rendering language while varying action, position, expression, and role-specific details.
